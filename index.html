Karınca

2025 to 2026 · Android, ~140 Kotlin files

A spaced repetition study app with a full virtual economy underneath it.

Built on Jetpack Compose with MVVM and Clean Architecture, Hilt for injection, Room locally and Supabase for sync. It runs an Anki style repetition engine with dynamic question generation, but the part I care about is the economy layered on top: XP, coins, levels, streaks, a theme shop and a paywall.

That is a game designer's problem wearing a study app's clothes. What makes someone open it tomorrow is not the flashcards, it is whether the loop gave them a reason to come back.

I wrote a UI specification targeting 60fps on 2020 and newer devices and WCAG 2.1 AA, then commissioned a full technical audit of my own codebase and worked from its findings rather than from my own opinion of the code. It scored 6.8 out of 10 and was blunt about weak test coverage and security gaps, which is exactly what made it useful.

  4. Room local, Supabase sync

  6. Dynamic question generation

Regulatory Monitoring Pipeline

2026 · BTS & Partners

An autonomous agent pipeline watching more than 100 public and regulatory sources, running unattended in production.

A collector agent retrieves every item, produces a summary with a relevance score and a topic tag, and writes it to a central database. Two publisher agents distribute the legislation and news bulletins by corporate email. Nobody touches it day to day.

I built the same workflow a second time on Microsoft Power Automate so the two could be compared properly. Querying that many sources and then running AI analysis over the results fell outside what the firm's license covered, so the agent build won: it runs inside existing subscription capacity with no additional infrastructure cost.

Separately I designed the evaluation methodology used on AI output there: a weighted accuracy benchmark for AI assisted contract review, and a Turkish to English translation quality comparison.

Built with

  1. Python, scheduled agents
  2. Supabase central store
  3. Streamlit for inspection
  4. Microsoft Graph for delivery
  5. Benchmarked against Power Automate
  6. Relevance scoring and tagging

Marketplace Reporting

2025 · Tradist Distribution

Five marketplace APIs, five different response shapes, one report the company actually reads.

Sales lived in five separate marketplace back offices. Every month somebody opened each one, exported what it offered in whatever format it offered, and pasted the pieces together by hand. The formats did not agree with each other: different field names, different date handling, different ideas of what counts as a return.

I pulled each channel through its own API, normalized the responses into one schema, and generated a single monthly report laid out the way the company reads it rather than the way any marketplace exports it. It removed more than five hours of manual gathering per month and, more usefully, removed the copy and paste errors that came with it.

What it does

  1. Five marketplace APIs, pulled on schedule
  2. Field mapping to one schema
  3. Currency, date and return normalization
  4. Company specific layout, not vendor layout
  5. One monthly report, 5+ hours saved
  6. Python and Google Apps Script

SMS Verification Relay

2025 · Tradist Distribution

Verification codes arriving on four phone lines, readable by the whole team without anyone holding a phone.

Marketplace back offices send a verification code by SMS every time somebody signs in. The phones sat in a drawer. Whoever needed to log in had to find the right handset, unlock it, read the code out loud, and hope it had not expired by the time they typed it.

I wrote an Android background service that listens for incoming messages, extracts the code, and writes it to a shared sheet the moment it lands. The service survives a device restart, so nobody has to remember to start it. Everyone on the team reads the same row at the same time, which is the whole point: the code stopped being something one person physically possessed.

It saved each person around twenty minutes a day. I found the need myself and built it on my own initiative; it was still running after I left.

How it works

  1. Android background service
  2. Listens for incoming SMS
  3. Extracts the code by pattern
  4. Writes to shared sheet in real time
  5. Survives device restart
  6. Whole team reads the same row

Dinlesen Yeter

2024 · generative music, trial project

Turkish literature and Ottoman history rewritten as songs for students preparing for the university entrance exam.

An experiment in producing generative content end to end, alone. I wrote each set of lyrics as a structured prompt for a generative music model, then iterated on takes until one held together as a song rather than a demo. Vocals and instrumentals were separated into stems so the mix could be corrected, cover art was generated and finished in Photoshop, and the video was cut and subtitled in Premiere Pro.

The tooling is the part worth showing. Generative models do not hold a metre, and checking syllable counts by hand does not scale past the second song, so I wrote a Turkish syllable counter and a small PyQt workspace around it, then wrote the lyrics against that instead.

Two tracks were published and three more were finished but never released. I stopped the project in 2024. The workflow it taught me is the one I still work in.

  3. Syllable counter (Python) for metre
  4. Vocal and instrumental stem separation

  6. Cover art, finished in Photoshop
  7. Edit and subtitle in Premiere Pro
